## $\color{purple}{\text{Part }\mathrm{I.}\text{ Problem }1\text{ : What we learned from Zeeman's paper}}$  

>$\text{Reference : E.C. Zeeman , \textit{The umbilic bracelet and the double-cusp catastrophe} , }$

$\text{The skills and techniques we learned :}$  
>$\color{green}{\mathrm{i.}\text{ (Lemma 3 in the paper) Every real cubic form in }(x,y)\text{ can be written in the complex form :}}$  
>$$\color{green}{f=\mathrm{Re}(\alpha z^3+\beta z^2\bar z)\;,\qquad (\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{C}^2}$$  
>$\color{green}{\mathrm{ii.}\text{ Eliminate by the resultant of two quadratics (just use hand computation enough).}}$  
>$\color{green}{\mathrm{iii.}\text{ (The trick for }\Sigma_2\text{ , from Ilya) If }\deg F=3\text{ , then }}$  
>$$\color{green}{\lambda\in\Sigma_2\iff F(x,y,\lambda)=Q_1Q_2+\alpha\;,\quad \deg Q_1=1,\;\deg Q_2=2}$$

## $\color{purple}{\text{Part }\mathrm{II.}\text{ our Hamiltonian in Zeeman's form}}$  

$\text{Our Hamiltonian is (MISSION 2) : }$  
$$
H=-\frac12(x^2+y^2)-\frac{B_2}{3}x^3+B_2xy^2+(B_1-A_1)x^2y-\Big(\frac{B_1}{3}+A_1\Big)y^3
$$  
$\text{We can write it in the complex form by lemma 3(i) : }$
$$  
\boxed{H=-\frac12|z|^2+\mathrm{Re}\!\big(\alpha z^3+\beta z^2\bar z\big)\;,\text{ with }\alpha=-\frac{B_2+iB_1}{3}\;,\quad \beta=iA_1}
$$  
$\text{In polar coordinates is : }$  
$$  
H=-\frac12r^2+r^3h(\theta)\;,\qquad h(\theta)=\mathrm{Re}\!\big(\alpha e^{3i\theta}+\beta e^{i\theta}\big)
$$

>$\text{From MISSION 3 we have :}$
>$$
>\Sigma_1:\ \exists\theta\;,\;h'(\theta)=h''(\theta)=0\;;\qquad
>\Sigma_2:\ \exists\,\theta_1\neq\theta_2\;,\;h'(\theta_1)=h'(\theta_2)=0\;,\;h(\theta_1)=h(\theta_2)>0
>$$

## $\color{purple}{\text{Part }\mathrm{III.}\text{ Hand computation of }\Sigma_1}$  

### $\color{red}{\text{Step 1 : }h'=0\text{ and }h''=0\text{ in complex form}}$  

$\text{Let }u=e^{i\theta}\text{ . Since }h=\mathrm{Re}(\alpha u^3+\beta u)\text{ we have : }$
$$
h'=\mathrm{Re}\big(i(3\alpha u^3+\beta u)\big)\;,\qquad h''=-\mathrm{Re}\big(9\alpha u^3+\beta u\big)
$$
$\text{Therefore : }$
$$
h'(\theta)=0\iff P:=3\alpha u^3+\beta u\in\mathbb{R}\;,\qquad
h''(\theta)=0\iff Q:=9\alpha u^3+\beta u\in i\mathbb{R}
$$

### $\color{red}{\text{Step 2 : simplify in one complex equation}}$
$P \in \mathbb{R},Q \in \mathbb{iR}$  
$\Rightarrow$  
$$
\begin{cases}
    P = \bar{P} \\
    Q = -\bar{Q}
\end{cases}
$$  
$\Rightarrow$  
$$
9\alpha u^3=\bar\beta\bar u-2\beta u
$$
$\text{Multiplying by }u\text{ and setting }w:=u^2\;(|w|=1)\text{ : }$
$$  
\color{red}{\qquad 9\alpha w^2+2\beta w=\bar\beta\qquad (|w|=1)}
$$  
### $\color{red}{\text{Step 3 : eliminate }w\text{ by the resultant}}$

$\text{Taking the conjugate of }(\star)\text{ and multiplying by }w^2\text{ we get two quadratics with a common root : }$
$$
P_1(w)=9\alpha w^2+2\beta w-\bar\beta\;,\qquad P_2(w)=-\beta w^2+2\bar\beta w+9\bar\alpha
$$  
$\text{Resultant of two quadratics polynomials }a_2w^2+a_1w+a_0\,,\;b_2w^2+b_1w+b_0\text{ is }(a_2b_0-a_0b_2)^2-(a_2b_1-a_1b_2)(a_1b_0-a_0b_1)\text{ . }$
$\text{Here : }a_2b_0-a_0b_2=81|\alpha|^2-|\beta|^2\,,\;a_2b_1-a_1b_2=18\alpha\bar\beta+2\beta^2\,,\;a_1b_0-a_0b_1=\overline{18\alpha\bar\beta+2\beta^2}\text{ . }$  
$\text{Then :}$  
$$
Res(P_1,P_2)=0
$$  
$\Rightarrow$  
$$
\color{red}{(\star)\qquad \big(81|\alpha|^2-|\beta|^2\big)^2=\big|\,18\alpha\bar\beta+2\beta^2\,\big|^2}
$$  

### $\color{red}{\text{Step 4 : back to }(A_1,B_1,B_2)}$  

$\text{Substituting }\alpha=-\frac{B_2+iB_1}{3}\,,\;\beta=iA_1\text{ : }$
$$
81|\alpha|^2-|\beta|^2=9(B_1^2+B_2^2)-A_1^2\;,\qquad
18\alpha\bar\beta+2\beta^2=-2A_1(3B_1+A_1)+6iA_1B_2
$$
$\text{So }(\star)\text{ becomes : }$
$$
\big(9(B_1^2+B_2^2)-A_1^2\big)^2=4A_1^2(3B_1+A_1)^2+36A_1^2B_2^2
$$
$\overset{simplify}{\Rightarrow}$  
$$
\boxed{\Sigma_1:\quad A_1^4+8A_1^3B_1+18A_1^2B_1^2+18A_1^2B_2^2-27B_1^4-54B_1^2B_2^2-27B_2^4=0}
$$  
$\color{green}{\text{Exactly the equation (*) of MISSION 2.}}$

## $\color{purple}{\text{Part }\mathrm{IV.}\text{ Hand computation of }\Sigma_2\text{ (the factorization trick)}}$

### $\color{red}{\text{Step 1 : the theorem (with proof)}}$

>$\color{blue}{\text{Theorem : let }F\text{ be a cubic polynomial . If }F\text{ has two different critical points }p_1\neq p_2\text{ on the same }}$
>$\color{blue}{\text{ level }\alpha\text{ , then }F-\alpha=L\,Q\text{ with }\deg L=1,\deg Q=2\text{ . Conversely , if }F-\alpha=LQ\text{ , then the two }}$
>$\color{blue}{\text{ intersection points of }L\cap Q\text{ are critical points of }F\text{ on the level }\alpha\text{ . }}$
>$\color{blue}{\text{Proof : a critical point on a level curve is a singular point of it , so the line }L\text{ through }p_1,p_2\text{ meets the }}$
>$\color{blue}{\text{ cubic curve }\{F=\alpha\}\text{ with multiplicity }\geq2+2=4>3\text{ ; by Bezout }L\text{ is a component . Conversely }}$
>$\color{blue}{dF=d(LQ)=Q\,dL+L\,dQ=0\text{ on }L\cap Q\text{ . }\blacksquare}$

$\color{red}{\text{Remark : for the REAL }\Sigma_2\text{ the two points of }L\cap Q\text{ must be real and different (inequalities !).}}$

### $\color{red}{\text{Step 2 : matching coefficients — there is no third plane}}$

$\text{Set }L=y-mx-c\text{ and }Q=q_{20}x^2+q_{11}xy+q_{02}y^2+q_{10}x+q_{01}y+q_{00}\text{ . Matching }H-\alpha=LQ$
$\text{ degree by degree gives : }$

$\text{(a) the cubic part : }(y-mx)\,|\,p\text{ , where }p\text{ is the cubic part of }H\text{ , i.e. }p(x,mx)\equiv0\text{ : }$
$$
(B_1+3A_1)m^3-3B_2m^2-3(B_1-A_1)m+B_2=0 \tag{vi}
$$

$\text{(b) the quadratic , linear and constant parts : }q_{10}=-\frac{q_{00}m}{c}\,,\;q_{01}=\frac{q_{00}}{c}\,,\;q_{20}=\frac{q_{00}m^2}{c^2}+\frac{1}{2c}\,,\;$
$q_{02}=\frac{q_{00}}{c^2}+\frac{1}{2c}\,,\;q_{11}=-\frac{2q_{00}m}{c^2}\text{ , which after comparing with the cubic coefficients of }p\text{ give : }$
$$
\color{red}{(\mathrm{v})\qquad B_2(1-3m^2)=m(3-m^2)(B_1-A_1)}
$$

$\color{blue}{\text{CASE }m=0\;:\;(\mathrm{vi})\text{ gives }B_2=0\;:\text{ the factorization }L=y-c\text{ lives on the plane }B_2=0\text{ . }}$

$\color{blue}{\text{CASE }B_2\neq0\;:\;(\mathrm{v})\text{ gives }B_1-A_1=\frac{B_2(1-3m^2)}{m(3-m^2)}\text{ ; substituting into }(\mathrm{vi})\text{ : }}$
$$
(B_1+3A_1)m^3=3B_2m^2+3(B_1-A_1)m-B_2=\frac{B_2m^2(1-3m^2)}{3-m^2}
\ \Rightarrow\ B_1+3A_1=\frac{B_2(1-3m^2)}{m(3-m^2)}=B_1-A_1
$$
$\color{blue}{\Rightarrow 4A_1=0\;:\text{ the factorization lives on the plane }A_1=0\text{ . }}$

$$
\color{red}{\therefore\ \Sigma_2\subseteq\{A_1=0\}\cup\{B_2=0\}\quad\text{ — NO third plane , proved by hand . }}
$$
